The AlignSynch model

See how feelings, wishes, and assumptions can drift apart.

First, notice where your views may differ. Then compare opinions on a scale, with room for answers between yes and no.

01 The Bread

Feel · Wish · Think · Guess

Four ways a gap can form

Notice the difference between what you feel or wish and what you think or guess about the other person.

  • FEELYou feel it is different than they feel.
  • WISHYou wish it to be different than they wish.
  • THINKYou think they feel it is different than they really feel.
  • GUESSYou guess they wish it to be different than they really wish.

Four potential pitfalls to notice in most relationships.

02 The Butter

Opinions are on a sliding scale

Opinions fall along a scale

An answer can be somewhere between yes and no. A small difference is still useful to notice.

YESNO

Somewhere in between

  • Actually, you FEEL it is only slightly different.
  • You WISH it to slowly change only a little bit.
  • They FEEL it is just a tad different.
  • They WISH it to be just a touch different.

What participants said

A clearer view can open a conversation.

These reflections describe seeing where things stand, finding a gentler way to talk, and recognizing that answers aren’t always just yes or no.

See where things stand

“This let me see where things stand and where we wish them to go in the future.”

Reach a state of agreement, harmony, and understanding. Progress—even slight progress—can give hope.

Open a softer conversation

“This works because it opened an easy soft communication channel for me!”
“It was hard for us to discuss these things. This opened our eyes to what we need to work on.”

Sometimes it is hard to know how to ask in a soft, non-confrontational way.

Find more than yes or no

“There would have been no other way for me to see all the angles of this relationship!”
“It allowed us to visit some important relationship aspects in a nice soft way to see what we need to work on.”

Answers can land partway in between. You may be closer than you think.

Why it matters

Why healthy relationships matter

At home or at work, relationships shape how we live and work together.

Less risk of losing connection

Misunderstandings can strain a partnership, customer relationship, or personal connection. Seeing a gap gives you something to address.

Stronger working relationships

Clearer expectations can make it easier to collaborate, care for one another, and solve problems together.

Emotional well-being

Supportive relationships can bring comfort, encouragement, and a sense of community.

Physical well-being

Healthy social connections contribute to overall well-being.

Less stress and anxiety

Support from colleagues or loved ones can help when life gets difficult.

Connection over time

Strong social support gives people a place to learn, grow, and stay connected.

A sense of belonging

Healthy relationships can ease isolation and help people feel part of a community.

More happiness

Positive relationships are an important part of life satisfaction.

Stronger self-esteem

Support from others can reinforce self-worth and confidence.

Support through hard times

Having someone to lean on can make challenges easier to face.

Better communication

Talking through expectations gives people practice expressing what matters to them.

A shared sense of purpose

Working toward understanding can give a relationship clearer direction.

Healthy relationships take effort, communication, and mutual respect—whether at work, in business, or in personal life.
