01How does AlignSynch work?

Choose a relationship template and invite the other person. You both answer the same Aspects privately, review your own answers, and accept when you are ready. The shared report opens after both of you accept.

What is an Aspect? →
02How do I get started?

Start with a template that fits your relationship. Create the relationship, add the other person's details, and send them the invitation. You can begin with a business template, or choose a personal relationship or friendship template.

Browse relationship templates →
03How does an invitation work?

The invitation is a unique link for the other person to join your relationship. It can be claimed once and remains available for seven days. Opening the link does not accept it; the invited person confirms before taking their seat. If it expires before they join, you can send a new invitation while the seat is unclaimed.

Read the invitation guide →
04Can I see the other person's answers while I answer?

No. Each person answers and reviews their own responses separately. The shared report stays locked until both people explicitly accept their answers.

See the two-person flow above ↑
05What is a Gap in the report?

A Gap is the distance between two comparable answers on an Aspect. It helps show where your views meet or differ. A Gap describes the answers to that topic; it does not judge the relationship or tell either person what to do.

Explore “Gap” in the glossary →
06What is the difference between Soft and Full?

The setting changes what counts as close enough in the report. Soft allows a one-point Gap; Full requires the answers to match. It does not change either person's answers.

See the definition →
07How do I update my account profile?

Open Account Settings from your signed-in menu. You can update your display name, handle, bio, and country there. Your sign-in email is shown on the page but cannot be changed from those settings.

Open Account Settings ↗
08A page looks out of date or a button does nothing. What should I try?

Reload the page and try again. If it still does not work, sign out and back in. If the problem continues, send a message below and include the page you were on and what you expected to happen.

Simple definitions for the words you see in templates, questions, and reports.

STARTING POINT

Relationship

A specific connection between two people, explored through shared topics and individual answers.

STARTING POINT

Template

A ready-made set of relationship topics and answer ranges that you can use to start a new relationship.

STARTING POINT

Aspect

A topic or characteristic of the relationship that you and the other person consider and answer questions about.

STARTING POINT

Value

A word or phrase at one end of an Aspect's scale. The two Values describe the range of possible answers.

STARTING POINT

Initiator

The person who starts a relationship in AlignSynch and invites the other person.

STARTING POINT

Counterparty

The app's label for the invited person. In everyday help copy, we call them “the other person.”

YOUR ANSWERS

Mode

A choice of which prompts appear. Basic includes Feel and Wish; Advanced adds Think and Guess.

YOUR ANSWERS

Opinion

Your answer for one Aspect, recorded on a scale. It reflects your perspective, not a right or wrong answer.

YOUR ANSWERS

Feel

How you feel the relationship is on this Aspect now.

YOUR ANSWERS

Wish

How you wish this Aspect could change over time.

YOUR ANSWERS · ADVANCED MODE

Think

What you think the other person feels about this Aspect now.

YOUR ANSWERS · ADVANCED MODE

Guess

What you guess the other person wishes could change over time.

YOUR REPORT

Gap

The distance between two comparable Opinions on an Aspect. It points to a difference in answers, not a judgment about the relationship.

YOUR REPORT

Soft and Full

Settings for what counts as close enough: Soft allows a one-point Gap; Full requires no Gap. The setting never changes the answers.

YOUR REPORT

Shared report

The view of both people's answers, available only after both have reviewed and accepted their responses.
