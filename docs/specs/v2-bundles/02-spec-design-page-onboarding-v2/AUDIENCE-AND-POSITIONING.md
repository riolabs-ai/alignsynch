The v2 catalog as drawn leads with family: Brother–Sister is the hero example, the "Most Popular" shortcut and the only detail page; the category order is Family, Couples, Friends, Work, Home. Either (a) reorder the catalog so Work templates (Co-Founders, Manager and Report) lead and the hero example uses a working relationship, or (b) record that the in-product catalog intentionally leads with what people pick most. The build team should not decide this; keep the category order data-driven so the call is a data change.
- **Pricing claims.** Only Single `$9.99 / month` comes from the spec. Family `$19.99` and Single Annual `$99` are placeholders. Do not publish plan prices until the owner confirms the tiers (this also satisfies "do not assert live pricing without evidence" below).

- **Crisis line.** Template pages end with "Not for crisis support. If someone is unsafe, reach out to local help first." Keep it.

## Canonical note (12 November 2026): AlignSynch audience and positioning

**Status:** Current messaging direction · 12 November 2026  
**Scope:** Public AlignSynch messaging and home-page redesign  
**Product source:** AlignSynch Project Brief, 4 November 2026

## Audience priority

### Primary: business and corporate relationships

Lead with people at work who want to compare expectations in a specific working relationship: colleagues, managers and staff, business partners, customers and vendors, and project teammates. Speak to the two people completing the assessment; describe corporate use as a context, not an enterprise feature set.

### Secondary: personal upskilling, relationships, and friendships

Address people who want to become more intentional in personal relationships and friendships. Frame personal upskilling as building reflection and relationship skills: consider how you feel, what you wish for, and what you understand about the other person's perspective. Avoid promising that AlignSynch will repair or improve a relationship.

### Small supporting note: counselors, therapists, and coaches

Keep this callout secondary and brief:

> Counselors, therapists, and coaches may use AlignSynch as an optional structure for reflection alongside their existing support. It is not a clinical assessment, diagnosis, therapy, or a substitute for professional judgment.

Do not present AlignSynch as a practitioner platform, clinical tool, treatment, or validated intervention. Counselors, therapists, and coaches are a supporting audience for the callout, not the page's primary target.

## Positioning

**One-line positioning**

> AlignSynch helps two people compare perspectives on a business or personal relationship through private answers and a shared report they open after both accept.

**Brand promise**

> A clear, shared starting point for the relationships that matter.

**Story**

Expectations at work can be hard to discuss when two people hold different pictures of what matters. AlignSynch lets them choose a relationship template, answer the same Aspects privately, and review a shared report after they have both completed and accepted their answers. The templates also cover personal relationships and friendships, giving people a way to reflect on their own expectations and the other person's perspective. The report organizes the conversation; it does not decide what the relationship means or what either person should do next.

## Value proposition

For business and corporate use, AlignSynch gives two people in a working relationship a structured way to compare views across shared topics. Each person answers privately, reviews their own answers, and explicitly accepts before the shared report opens. For personal use, the same flow supports reflection on relationships and friendships. The report makes each perspective visible by Aspect without reducing the relationship to one score.

### Message pillars

1. **Business first:** workplace and corporate relationships lead the page and primary CTA.
2. **Personal growth next:** personal relationships and friendships are clear secondary paths.
3. **Two private perspectives:** both people answer the same Aspects in separate participant seats.
4. **A clear privacy gate:** the shared report opens after both people answer and explicitly accept.
5. **A conversation aid:** AlignSynch organizes answers; people decide what they mean and what comes next.

## Reusable profile copy

**Short description**

> AlignSynch helps two people compare expectations in a working relationship, with personal relationship and friendship templates too.

**Company profile**

> AlignSynch is a relationship-assessment product for people who want a clearer view of a business or personal relationship. Choose a template, invite the other person, and answer the same topics privately. After both people review and accept their answers, AlignSynch opens a shared report that organizes each perspective for discussion. Business and corporate relationships lead the experience, with templates for personal relationships and friendships as well.

**App profile**

> Compare expectations in a working relationship. Choose a template, invite the other person, and answer the same Aspects privately. Your shared report opens after you both review and accept. Use it to see where your views meet or differ, then decide what to discuss next. Personal relationship and friendship templates are available for reflection too. AlignSynch does not diagnose relationships or assign one relationship score.

## Calls to action

- **Primary:** Explore business relationship templates.
- **Secondary:** Explore personal relationships and friendships.
- **Supporting:** See how the two-person process works.

Write directly to the two people involved: “your relationship,” “the other person,” “answer privately,” and “when you are both ready.” Do not frame the visitor as a practitioner setting up someone else's assessment.

## Copy and claim boundaries

- Preserve the current product sequence: one person starts and invites the other; both answer the same Aspects privately; each reviews and explicitly accepts; the shared report opens after both accept.
- Keep corporate use focused on working relationships, one two-person relationship at a time. Do not imply unverified enterprise administration, analytics, or deployment features.
- Do not call the product a quiz, diagnosis, clinical instrument, therapy service, or practitioner platform.
- Do not claim treatment, guaranteed relationship improvement, reduced stress, better health, validated efficacy, or a composite score.
- Describe personal growth as reflection and skill-building, not guaranteed behavior change.
- Keep counselors, therapists, and coaches in one brief secondary callout with the professional-boundary language above.
- Do not assert customer proof, founder story, legal entity details, live pricing, or production availability without separate evidence.
- Use “Aspect,” “participant,” “private answer,” “explicitly accept,” and “shared report” consistently.

## Where this direction applies

Use this hierarchy in the static home redesign, private brand profile, marketing-page implementation brief, page-copy fixtures, template-catalogue introductions, and future public-page specs. It defines public audience and copy; it does not change the application's current workflow or account model.
